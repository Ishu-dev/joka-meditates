The Month of Restraint

Saavan asks you to do less. That turns out to be the hard part.
Issue 03

Shravan arrives with the rain and a strange instruction: eat lighter, sit longer, want less. In a term where everything is asking for more of you, that lands oddly.

The month isn't asking you to add a practice.
It's asking you to subtract noise.

Below: why the season works this way, what to eat and skip, two asanas, a breathing visualization, playlists to sit with, and an argument for being bored on purpose.

The Season

Why Saavan Works This Way

The monsoon logic is older than any of us and fairly practical underneath the ritual. Humidity climbs, sunlight drops, and digestion genuinely slows. Traditional practice reads that as a signal: this is not the season for heavy food, hard travel, or scattered energy.

So the month turns inward instead. Lighter meals. More chanting. More sitting. Less of everything that pulls outward. Shravan is the one month in the calendar that treats restraint as the practice itself, rather than as preparation for something else.

You do not need to hold any particular belief for the structure to be useful. A month of deliberately doing less, right as the term gets heaviest, is a good idea on its own terms.

Observance

Fasting, Honestly

Saavan fasting is observance before it is anything else. Mondays are the traditional day, and the forms people keep vary widely.

The common forms

Some keep a full fast on Mondays. Some eat one simple meal. Some only drop grains, or salt, or anything non-vegetarian for the month. All of these count. There is no leaderboard here.

What it is actually for

The point is not the empty stomach. It is noticing how loudly the body asks, how quickly the mind negotiates, and choosing anyway. That noticing is the practice. The food is just the setting.

A softer version

If a food fast isn't for you, keep the shape and change the object. A day without screens after 9pm. A day without complaining. Same muscle, different weight.

Please skip food fasting entirely if you have any medical reason to, are on medication that needs food, or have any history of disordered eating. Talk to a doctor rather than to us. The screen version above works just as well.

Food

Eating With the Weather

Principles, not a plan. Monsoon digestion is slower and the campus water table is doing what it does every year.

  1. 01 Warm over cold, cooked over rawSalads and cold food sit heavier this season than they look. Cooked and warm moves easier.
  2. 02 Careful with leafy greensTraditionally avoided through Shravan, and the reasoning holds up: they hold moisture and contamination through a wet month.
  3. 03 Ginger, turmeric, black pepper, ajwainThe standard monsoon kitchen. They earn their place by helping a slow gut along.
  4. 04 Water that has been boiledThe least spiritual line in this issue and possibly the most useful one.
  5. 05 Stop a little before fullThe oldest instruction in the book, and the one nobody follows. Try it for a week.
The Argument

Be Bored On Purpose

Here is the part that will feel wrong.

Every gap in your day is currently filled. The walk to class, the queue at the mess, the ninety seconds before a lecture starts, all of it goes to a screen. Nothing in that is unusual and nobody is being scolded here. But it means the mind has not been genuinely unoccupied in a long time, possibly years.

Idle and distracted are not the same thing. Scrolling is not rest, it is a different kind of work. Actual idleness, sitting with nothing happening and not reaching for anything, is where attention slowly repairs itself. It feels unbearable for the first few minutes and then it does not.

The practical case is straightforward. The ability to hold attention on one thing for a long stretch is getting rarer every year. Whatever you end up doing after this campus, that will be worth more than it is today, precisely because fewer people will have it.

So this month, leave one gap empty. Do not fill the queue. Do not fill the walk. Let it be boring on purpose and see what your head does with the room.

Body

Two Asanas for a Wet Month

Both are seated, both help a sluggish monsoon digestion, and both can be done in a hostel room without moving furniture.

Vajrasana

Kneel, sit back on the heels, spine tall, hands on thighs. The one pose traditionally done straight after eating rather than before. Five to ten minutes post-meal is the classic prescription for exactly the heaviness this season brings.

Supta Matsyendrasana (supine twist)

Lie on your back, draw one knee across the body, let the head turn the other way, hold and breathe. A gentle wring for the middle of the body. Do both sides, a minute or two each, and it works well right before sleep.

None of this replaces a proper workout. Restraint this month is about food and noise, not about skipping movement, warm up before anything strenuous and keep the regular exercise going.
